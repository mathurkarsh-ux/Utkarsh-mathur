🧠 Deep Dive

Artificial Intelligence
is rewriting everything.

Artificial General Intelligence is the most consequential thing humanity may ever build. Here's why a naval officer in the middle of the ocean can't stop thinking about it.

First — what even is AGI?

Today's AI — Claude, GPT-4, Gemini — is narrow AI. Extraordinarily capable at specific tasks. Writing, coding, analysis. But fundamentally a very sophisticated pattern matcher trained on human text.

AGI — Artificial General Intelligence — is something categorically different. A system that can learn, reason, and solve problems across any domain at human level or beyond. Not just answering questions. Actually thinking. Planning. Discovering. Creating.

Most researchers believe we will build it. The debate is when — and what happens after.

Why a Naval Officer pays attention

"When you spend months at sea, you develop a certain relationship with forces larger than yourself. The ocean humbles you constantly. AGI feels like that — something so vast and inevitable that ignoring it seems almost disrespectful."
🌊
The ocean teaches scale
At sea you understand viscerally that the world is enormous and indifferent. AGI is the same — a force of nature scale event. I find people who haven't stared at open ocean for weeks tend to underestimate it.
⏳
Time alone gives clarity
Six months contracts. Long night watches. No social media distractions. That kind of solitude lets you think deeply about big things. AGI kept rising to the top, every single time.

How I think it unfolds

2024–2026
NOW
Narrow AI becomes indispensable
AI assistants handle writing, coding, research, planning. Most knowledge workers use AI daily without thinking about it. This is already happening.
2026–2028
Agentic AI takes over entire workflows
AI agents don't just answer questions — they run processes end to end. Software engineers, analysts, writers work alongside AI that does 80% of execution.
2028–2032
The blurry line to AGI
Systems that can learn new domains, reason across problems, and self-improve in limited ways. We may cross the AGI threshold without a clear announcement moment.
2032+
Acceleration we cannot predict
Once AGI systems can improve AI research itself, the pace of change becomes genuinely difficult to model. This is where honest uncertainty begins.

Three things I believe

🎯
It's coming. The only debate is when.
The trajectory of capability improvement over the last 5 years makes the "it won't happen" position very hard to defend. The serious debate is timeline — not possibility.
⚖️
Alignment is the most important problem
Building something smarter than us that does what we actually want — not just what we say — is extraordinarily hard. Anthropic's Constitutional AI approach genuinely interests me.
🌍
Most people are not paying attention
The gap between what's happening in AI labs and what most people understand is enormous and growing. That gap feels dangerous. Which is partly why I write.

"The most dangerous assumption is that the future will look like the past — only faster."
