Most people imagine life at sea as romantic. Long horizons, sunsets, adventure. That part is real. But the honest version also includes six-month stretches away from family, four-hours-on four-hours-off watch schedules, and weeks where the most interesting event is a passing ship on the radar.

For the first several years, I read a lot. Thought a lot. The ocean gives you something cities rarely do — uninterrupted time with your own thoughts. No traffic. No meetings. No social noise. Just the watch, the sea, and your mind.

Then Starlink arrived on our vessel. And everything changed.

When Starlink arrived

"The moment I had real internet in the middle of the Indian Ocean was the moment I fell into AI. I started with ChatGPT out of curiosity. Six months later I was reading research papers on Constitutional AI on night watch."
📡
Before Starlink
Slow, expensive VSAT. Email only. No video calls. No streaming. The ship's library and each other's company. The ocean was genuinely disconnected from the world.
🛰️
After Starlink
Broadband in the middle of the ocean. Video calls home. Real-time news. And suddenly — access to the most fascinating technological revolution in human history, in real time, from a ship's bridge.

Why ocean perspective matters for AI

🌊
Comfort with uncertainty
At sea you make decisions with incomplete information constantly. Weather forecasts are probabilistic. AI works the same way. Sailors are unusually comfortable with probabilistic thinking.
🧭
Long-horizon thinking
Navigation is inherently about where you'll be in 12 hours, not right now. That same long-horizon mindset makes thinking about AI's 10-year trajectory feel natural, not abstract.
⚠️
Risk awareness
The sea teaches you that underestimating powerful forces is fatal. That lesson applies directly to how I think about AI safety and alignment. Not fear — respect.
🔭
Distance gives clarity
Being physically removed from Silicon Valley hype cycles means I read the primary sources, not the commentary. The ocean is a surprisingly good filter for signal vs. noise.

"The ocean doesn't care how much you know. It only responds to how well you pay attention."
